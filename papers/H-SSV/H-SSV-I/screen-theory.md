# Part I — C4 quantum causal global screen

## C4.1 Global screen, topological sites and patches

The global screen is one directed acyclic causal network `Sigma`, not one
equal-time material shell. A topological screen site `e` carries a finite
Hilbert factor,

\[
\mathcal H_S=\bigotimes_{e\in\Sigma}\mathcal H_e,
\qquad d_e=\dim\mathcal H_e<\infty.
\]

The causal partial order is physical. A coordinate time or an observer's
spacelike cut is only a linear representation of that order. A cut is an
antichain of mutually spacelike sites; no cut defines a physical global rest
frame.

An effective galaxy, host/satellite or cluster screen is a subalgebra
`A(P)` generated by a set of sites, not a new boundary. Two effective patches
may share an entangled state or a common mode. Their state can therefore be
correlated or nearly uniform without allowing either patch to signal to the
other outside the causal order. Capacity and energy are counted once in the
global tensor product.

## C4.2 Two capacities at a screen site and a particle

A topological screen site has both state capacity and update capacity:

\[
C_e^{\rm state}=\ln d_e\quad\text{nats},
\qquad
\nu_e^{\max}=1/\tau_e\quad\text{s}^{-1}.
\]

A moving particle is likewise a finite local system,

\[
C_p^{\rm state}=\ln d_p,
\qquad
\nu_p^{\max}=1/\tau_p.
\]

These are different quantities. `C_state` bounds the distinguishable local
state and retained correlations. `nu_max` bounds the number of local update
gates per proper time. Present content, active write rate and retained memory
are, respectively,

\[
N_P=S(\rho_P\|\sigma_P),\qquad
J_P={dN_{\rm gates}\over d\tau},\qquad
M_{P:B}=I(P:B),
\]

with units `1`, `s^-1` and `1`. Neither entropy nor gate count is assigned an
energy without an explicit physical carrier.

## C4.3 Bilateral relocation writes and availability

A bulk particle has a relational screen address: the site algebra with which
its state can interact at a causal event. Relocation means that this adjacency
changes along the particle's worldline. The event applies one local gate
`W_v` to the particle, carrier and addressed screen factor. It consumes one
update slot on both ledgers:

\[
\sum_e j_{pe}+j_p^{\rm int}\le\nu_p^{\max},
\qquad
\sum_p j_{pe}+j_e^{\rm self}\le\nu_e^{\max}.
\]

The same relocation is not counted as two physical events; `j_pe` is one
bilateral rate appearing in both conservation ledgers. It is defined per
proper time and causal adjacency, not by coordinate velocity.

For active demand at a screen site,

\[
j_e=\sum_pj_{pe}+j_e^{\rm self},
\qquad q_e={j_e\over\nu_e^{\max}},
\qquad 0\le q_e\le1.
\]

The site's remaining universal update availability is therefore

\[
\boxed{A_e=1-q_e},
\qquad \Phi_e=c^2\ln A_e.
\]

Every locally coupled clock sees the same service fraction. A moving
particle's internal clock also draws on its own budget: relocation gates and
internal updates satisfy the first inequality above. This is capacity
allocation, not friction or an unrecorded energy loss.

At `q_e=1` the topological site is saturated and supplies no unused update
service. Whether a physical system can reach that endpoint is deferred; the
foundation fixes the limiting behavior.

## C4.4 Bulk-to-screen map without a preferred foliation

The encoding map is a causal isometry built from local gates,

\[
V=\mathop{\prod}_{v\in G}^{\rm causal}W_v,
\qquad W_v^\dagger W_v=1.
\]

If `v` and `w` are spacelike-incomparable, their gates act on disjoint local
factors and

\[
[W_v,W_w]=0.
\]

Consequently every linear extension of the same causal partial order produces
the same `V`. A Lorentz observer may order two spacelike writes differently;
the screen state and every observable prediction remain identical.

Every gate edge satisfies `Delta x/Delta tau<=c`. A local operation `U_P` on
one patch cannot alter the reduced state of a spacelike remote patch `R`:

\[
\rho_R'={\rm Tr}_{\bar R}
[(U_P\otimes1)\rho(U_P^\dagger\otimes1)]=\rho_R.
\]

It may change a global connected correlation. Observers can reveal that
change only after bringing measurement records into causal contact. This is
the precise sense in which the screen has both local causality and a global
state.

## C4.5 Energy-momentum and information ledger

Each local write gate intertwines the additive local translation generator,

\[
[W_v,P^\mu_p+P^\mu_e+P^\mu_{\rm carrier}]=0.
\]

A complete-state SWAP between equal representations is the minimal explicit
example: an occupation and all its four-momentum labels move from the particle
factor into the screen factor while their sum remains fixed. Address-record
gates may act on degenerate registers and commute with the same generator.

The product `V` is unitary/isometric, hence the global von Neumann entropy is
unchanged. Screen or particle reduced entropy may increase because the gate
entangles them. The apparent open-system information flow is therefore backed
by a closed global ledger, as Paper III requires.

## C4.6 Memory, saturation and Paper III

For a patch of finite sites,

\[
S(\rho_P)\le\sum_{e\in P}\ln d_e.
\]

Repeated stationary or periodic isolated updates may move correlations and
make local observables oscillate, but neither state content nor gate throughput
can grow past its finite bound. At full local state/update capacity, new writes
must wait or be redistributed by a causal energy-conserving gate to other
available factors. The global state retains the information; a reduced patch
may forget it.

All relocation writes change a relational screen record. Only the part made
inaccessible by the declared trace/coarse graining raises Paper III's wake
entropy. A reversible SWAP can therefore be a physical screen write with no
global entropy production, while an entangling write raises reduced entropy.

## C4.7 Positive combination and shared-state prediction

Let `n_e` be a positive record projector. For a patch,

\[
Q_P=\sum_{e\in P}n_e\ge0.
\]

The expectation of `Q_P` is identical for symmetric and antisymmetric
one-record superpositions. Relative phase can alter correlations but cannot
turn positive gate demand or record occupation into a negative source.

C4 makes two pre-gravity predictions that were not selected from galaxy
residuals:

1. shared patches may have nonzero connected correlations, but a unilateral
   perturbation leaves the remote marginal unchanged until causal contact;
2. particles addressed to one topological site share one bounded update
   service. Their availability is correlated, additive below capacity and
   collectively saturating/subadditive at capacity, never phase-cancelling.

These are falsifiers of the foundation. They do not yet give a gravitational
Green function, coupling magnitude, spatial metric, rotation law, lensing law
or cosmology. Those remain H-SSV-II and later work.

# Part II — C3 classical membrane control (rejected)

The following classical construction remains useful because it closes five
gates and isolates why a material membrane rest frame is the wrong ontology
for the global-state idea.

## 1. Physical and effective screens

The candidate postulates one global timelike worldvolume

\[
\Sigma_R \simeq \mathbb R\times S^2,
\qquad h_{ab}\;\text{with signature }(-,+,+),
\]

at finite areal radius `R` about a center worldline `Gamma_S`. It carries a
future-directed unit tangent `u_S^a` and spatial projector

\[
p_{ab}=h_{ab}+u^S_a u^S_b/c^2.
\]

The finite-radius surface is necessary to pose a causal initial-boundary-value
problem. A limit `R -> infinity` is not assumed to exist.

A galaxy, satellite or cluster does **not** own another fundamental screen.
An effective patch is a non-negative window `W_P(y)` on `Sigma_R`; an
effective mode is a basis coefficient

\[
q_n(t)=\int_{\Sigma_t}d^2y\sqrt p\,f_n(y)q(y,t).
\]

Windows may overlap, but capacity and energy are integrated once on the global
screen. Patch sums are bookkeeping identities only when the windows form a
partition of unity. This prevents shared host/satellite modes from
double-counting a second screen.

**Postulated, not derived:** `Sigma_R`, `R`, `Gamma_S`, `u_S`, its topology and
the mode basis.

## 2. Capacity, content, writes and memory

Use nats as the dimensionless information unit.

| object | definition | units |
|---|---|---:|
| local capacity | `C(P)=integral_P c_* dA` | 1 |
| capacity density | `c_*>0` | m^-2 |
| encoded content | `N(P)=integral_P n dA` | 1 |
| content density | `n` | m^-2 |
| relocation write rate | `J_w(P)=integral_P j_w dA` | s^-1 |
| write-rate density | `j_w>=0` | m^-2 s^-1 |
| retained memory | `M(P)=integral_P m dA` | 1 |
| memory density | `m` | m^-2 |

These are not synonyms. In particular, an entropy or a number of nats is not
an energy until multiplied by an independently declared energy scale.

Let `Y_t:x -> y` be the screen address of a bulk degree of freedom and `K(y|x)`
its normalized coarse-grained address kernel,

\[
K\ge0,\qquad \int_{\Sigma_t}dA_y\,K(y|x)=1,
\qquad [K]={\rm m}^{-2}.
\]

For bulk rest-energy density `epsilon_0` and one declared encoding energy
`E_w`,

\[
n(y,t)={1\over E_w}\int_{B_t}d^3x\,K(y|x)\epsilon_0(x,t).
\]

The total write rate splits as

\[
j_w=j_{\rm rel}+j_{\rm irr}.
\]

`j_rel` counts every change of address `Y_t`, including reversible motion.
`j_irr` counts only the part that becomes inaccessible after the declared
coarse graining. Thus every relocation may rewrite the screen without every
relocation being an entropy-producing Paper-III event.

The retained reduced-state memory obeys

\[
D_t m=j_{\rm irr}(1-m/c_*)-m/\tau_m,
\qquad D_t=u_S^aD_a,quad \tau_m>0.
\]

For constant `j_irr`,

\[
m_\infty={c_*j_{\rm irr}\tau_m\over c_*+j_{\rm irr}\tau_m},
\]

so `0<=m<=c_*` is invariant and no age-proportional load is retained. The law
is a reduced-state Markov approximation, not a fundamental violation of the
closed dynamics.

## 3. Bulk-to-screen transfer

The kernel must be a retarded response of a local carrier, not an instantaneous
projection:

\[
K(y,t|x,t')=0\quad\text{unless}\quad
(y,t)\in J^+(x,t'),
\]

and every carrier characteristic speed is `c_B<=c`. Matter, carrier and screen
exchange a local four-force density,

\[
\nabla_\mu T_{\rm matter}^{\mu\nu}=-f^\nu,
\qquad
\nabla_\mu T_{\rm carrier}^{\mu\nu}=f^\nu-f_\Sigma^\nu,
\qquad
D_aT_{\rm screen}^{ab}=e^b{}_{\nu}f_\Sigma^\nu-f_{\rm bath}^b.
\]

With the bath equation added, the sum vanishes. This is the local conservation
condition a microscopic carrier action must realize.

The issue-226 candidate does **not** specify such an action uniquely. It fixes
only positivity, normalization, causal support and the exchange ledger. This
freedom is harmless for the analytic controls but fatal to the claim that a
physical global screen has been identified.

## 4. Screen-relative source

Let `U^mu` be a matter four-velocity and let the global screen flow be extended
to the bulk as `u_S^mu`. The scalar

\[
\gamma_{US}=-U_\mu u_S^\mu/c^2,\qquad r=\gamma_{US}-1\ge0
\]

is invariant under a passive coordinate change. The positive energy source
conjugate to the load is

\[
s(y)=\int d^3x\,K(y|x)\epsilon_0(x)
       [g_n+g_r r(x)]+E_m m(y),
\]

where `g_n>=0`, `g_r>0` and `[E_m]=J`. Consequently `[s]=J m^-2` and ordinary
positive contributions add as intensities; phases cannot cancel them.

At small screen-relative speed,

\[
s_{\rm relocation}-s_{\rm rest}
= {g_r\over2}\int d^3x\,K\epsilon_0{v_{\rm rel}^2\over c^2}
+O(v_{\rm rel}^4/c^4).
\]

This is an independent falsifier: at fixed rest content, the load must grow
with motion relative to the physical screen. Setting `g_r=0` removes the
prediction but also abandons the load-bearing statement that relocation loads
the screen.

## 5. Causal load and regulated response

The dimensionless load `q(y,t)` has surface Lagrangian density

\[
\mathcal L_q={I_*\over2}(D_tq)^2
-{T_*\over2}p^{ab}D_aqD_bq
-{K_*\over2}q^2-{\lambda_*\over4}q^4+q s,
\]

with

\[
[I_*]=M,\quad [T_*]=ML^2T^{-2},\quad
[K_*]=[\lambda_*]=[s]=MT^{-2}.
\]

Tracing a local oscillator reservoir gives the effective equation

\[
I_*D_t^2q+\Gamma_*D_tq-T_*\Delta_Sq
+K_*q+\lambda_*q^3=s,
\]

where `Gamma_*>0` and `[Gamma_*]=MT^-1`. The principal speed is

\[
c_S=\sqrt{T_*/I_*}\le c.
\]

For a linearized mode of frequency `omega`, the forced-response denominator is

\[
(K_*+T_*k^2-I_*\omega^2)^2+(\Gamma_*\omega)^2>0,
\]

so every finite-frequency bounded periodic source has bounded response. The
quartic term bounds large static excursions. A closed global `S^2` needs no
edge condition; an effective patch inherits the global solution and may not
choose an independent boundary condition.

## 6. Energy ledger

For the reduced equation, screen energy and flux obey

\[
\partial_t e_q+D_aS_q^a=sD_tq-\Gamma_*(D_tq)^2.
\]

The matter/carrier sector supplies `s D_t q`; the reservoir receives
`Gamma_*(D_tq)^2`. Therefore

\[
\partial_t(e_{\rm matter}+e_{\rm carrier}+e_q+e_{\rm bath})
+\boldsymbol\nabla\cdot
(\mathbf S_{\rm matter}+\mathbf S_{\rm carrier}+\mathbf S_q+
\mathbf S_{\rm bath})=0.
\]

Damping without the receiving reservoir fails this identity. Calling the
missing term "information" would not conserve energy.

## 7. Clock response

The fixed composition-independent response is

\[
A(q)=e^{-\alpha q}>0,\qquad
\Phi=c^2\ln A=-\alpha c^2q,
\qquad \alpha>0.
\]

Every suitable clock is postulated to couple to this same `A`. This supplies a
universal time-sector map only. It does not derive free-fall motion, a spatial
metric, lensing or the value of `alpha`; those are outside H-SSV-I.

## 8. Paper-III reduced-state connection

Let the full matter + carrier + screen + reservoir state evolve unitarily and
let

\[
\rho_S={\rm Tr}_{\rm matter,carrier,bath}\rho_{\rm total}.
\]

For a declared coarse-graining channel `R` and its local-equilibrium reference
`sigma_S`, define the screen wake contribution

\[
W_S=D(\mathcal R\rho_S\|\sigma_S).
\]

This relative entropy is dimensionless and non-negative. Its change is a
reduced-state statement; the global information and energy ledgers remain
closed. `j_irr` may increase or redistribute `W_S`. A reversible address change
can have `j_rel>0` while `Delta W_S=0`, matching Paper III's explicit exclusion
of reversible relabellings from entropy production.

No equality `m=W_S/dA` is derived. Treating the phenomenological memory density
as Paper III's entropy would require a microscopic channel decomposition and
calibration that this candidate does not supply.

## 9. Why C3 fails

`gamma_US` is a scalar only after `u_S` exists; `K` is causal only after the
screen address map exists. The equations do not determine `Gamma_S`, `R`,
`u_S`, the extension of `u_S` into the bulk or `K`. Different choices give
different write rates, preferred-frame anisotropies and retardation times.

Thus C3 is coordinate-covariant but not observer-complete. Its missing
structure is exactly the physical structure the screen hypothesis was meant to
define. The theory fails F3, so it is not eligible to seed H-SSV-II.
