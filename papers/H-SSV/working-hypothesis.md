# Screen-Update Gravity: Working Hypothesis and Resumption Record

**Status:** speculative programme note, not an established SSV result

**Recorded:** 2026-08-04

**Purpose:** preserve the complete reasoning chain, its inherited results, its
known failures, and the next calculations so the investigation can be resumed
without reconstructing it from conversation.

## 1. Motivation

Paper IV's original upstream mechanism for producing the gravitational
potential, coherent pressure-wave interference between material sources, is
falsified as written. Its downstream chain survives conditionally: given a
potential $\Phi$, reduced update availability produces gravitational time
dilation, and a spatial gradient in that delay produces free-fall acceleration.
The missing object is therefore a physical, sign-definite source and carrier of
$\Phi$.

The present hypothesis reopens that gap using a holographic information screen.
The screen is not decorative language and is not identified with the previously
failed radiated-wave mechanism. It is proposed as the physical system that
encodes matter, receives continual state updates, retains distributed
information, and deforms under that load.

The motivating empirical clue is an exploratory rotation-curve analysis of
eleven galaxies shared by the 2023 logarithmic-SVT paper and SPARC. After fitting
all 32 hierarchical reductions of the published equation, the dominant radial
term was a cored logarithmic-halo law. A logarithmic potential is also the
static Green-function behaviour naturally associated with two-dimensional
propagation. This is a structural coincidence worth testing, not evidence for
the screen mechanism.

## 2. Inherited structure and present status

### 2.1 Paper III: change, writing, wake, and entropy

Paper III supplies the ontology:

- physical time is the local change-rate of the state $\Psi$;
- events change the present physical state;
- changes can deposit distributed wake information;
- coarse-graining over that distributed record produces wake entropy;
- the global microscopic dynamics can remain conservative and reversible even
  when the matter subsystem cannot naturally gather the screen/environmental
  record and run the inverse history.

Paper III currently distinguishes wake-writing events from purely reversible
relabelings. The new hypothesis is stronger: **every physical relocation of
encoded matter rewrites the screen and transfers path/state information to
screen degrees of freedom.** If adopted, Paper III must explain why apparently
reversible relocation is open-system irreversible after the screen is traced
out. This is a proposed extension, not a result already derived in Paper III.

### 2.2 Paper IV: update capacity and gravity downstream of $\Phi$

Paper IV already supplies most of the response chain:

- translation is updating: carrying a defect through the medium rewrites the
  grains along its path;
- internal ticking and translation draw from one update budget;
- local loading reduces update availability;
- availability is the proper-time rate,
  \[
  A \equiv \frac{d\tau}{dt};
  \]
- in the weak field,
  \[
  A=1+\frac{\Phi}{c^2},
  \qquad
  -g_{00}=A^2\simeq 1+\frac{2\Phi}{c^2};
  \]
- a gradient in update delay produces acceleration,
  \[
  \mathbf g=-\nabla\Phi=-c^2\nabla\ln A
  \]
  at the corresponding level of approximation.

This is the intended meaning of **drag** in this note: universal clock-rate or
update drag, not an ordinary frictional force. A local observer's clock,
chemistry, and mechanical processes slow together. A uniform reduction of $A$
produces no force; the spatial gradient produces gravity.

Paper IV does **not** have a surviving derivation of the source of $\Phi$. Its
mutual-radiation source is falsified. It also does not derive the complete
spatial metric: the bare medium produced the clock/time half but failed the
required spatial half of light deflection. Those negative results remain in
force.

## 3. Core hypothesis

The proposed causal chain is

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\text{matter content and relocation}
&\longrightarrow \text{screen writes},\\
\text{screen writes}
&\longrightarrow \text{information transfer and screen load},\\
\text{screen load}
&\longrightarrow \text{reduced local update availability},\\
\nabla A
&\longrightarrow \text{gravity},\\
\text{spatial screen deformation}
&\longrightarrow \text{lensing}.
\end{aligned}}
\]

The hypothesis contains the following working postulates.

1. **Physical encoding.** Matter and fields inside a screen boundary are
   represented by physical degrees of freedom of the screen.
2. **State-content loading.** Encoded mass-energy contributes to the screen's
   present information content and static deformation.
3. **Relocation writes.** Any relocation of encoded matter changes the screen
   state. This statement is relative to the screen encoding, not to an
   arbitrarily chosen observer.
4. **Information transfer.** Relocation disperses information into screen
   degrees of freedom. It can be globally retained while becoming inaccessible
   to the reduced matter description, producing coarse-grained entropy.
5. **Finite update availability.** Screen degrees of freedom have finite local
   update capacity. Existing content and active writes reduce the update rate
   available to embedded processes.
6. **Time dilation is update drag.** The availability field $A=d\tau/dt$ is the
   local clock-rate response to screen loading.
7. **Gravity is differential update drag.** Matter and waves follow the spatial
   gradient of $A$, equivalently of $\Phi=c^2\ln A$.
8. **Positive combination.** Independent sources contribute through
   information/load, not coherent wave amplitude. Ordinary source contributions
   therefore do not disappear through phase cancellation.
9. **Memory with regulation.** The screen retains some history, but its response
   must relax, overwrite, redistribute, or saturate. Otherwise gravity would
   grow without bound with the number of completed orbits or the age of a
   galaxy.
10. **Collective modes.** Systems whose screen responses overlap or become
    dynamically correlated may excite a shared screen mode. A "galaxy screen"
    or "cluster screen" is provisionally an effective correlated patch or mode
    of the universal screen, not necessarily a separate fundamental boundary.

## 4. Quantities that must not be conflated

Four different quantities are present:

1. **Capacity:** the maximum information/update burden a screen region can
   support. Holographic area scaling is a candidate capacity law.
2. **Content:** the information currently encoded by matter and fields.
3. **Write rate:** the rate at which relocation and internal rearrangement
   change the encoding.
4. **Memory:** the part of prior writing retained in the present screen state.

The working statement "the screen is proportional to its information content"
must eventually specify which screen observable is proportional to which of
these quantities. Area-capacity proportionality alone does not derive
deformation, time dilation, or gravity.

## 5. Candidate mathematical skeleton

Let $\sigma(\mathbf y,t)$ denote a screen-load field on screen coordinates
$\mathbf y$, and let $\mathcal J_{\rm write}$ be a source functional of encoded
matter and its relocation. A causal response could take the schematic form

\[
\left[
\frac{1}{c_s^2}\partial_t^2
+\frac{1}{c_s^2\tau_s}\partial_t
-\nabla_s^2
\right]\sigma
+\mathcal N(\sigma)
=\kappa\,\mathcal J_{\rm write},
\]

where $c_s\leq c$ is the screen signal speed, $\tau_s$ controls retained
memory, and $\mathcal N$ provides any physically required saturation. This is
only a model class; none of its coefficients or nonlinearities is yet derived.

The screen-to-clock response is

\[
A=F(\sigma),
\qquad
\Phi=c^2\ln A,
\qquad
\mathbf g=-c^2\nabla\ln A.
\]

The weak-field limit requires $F(\sigma)\simeq1+\Phi/c^2$. Universality
requires the same $F$ for every suitable clock and material composition.

### 5.1 Two-dimensional static limit

For a massless static screen response,

\[
\nabla_s^2\sigma=-\kappa\mathcal J,
\]

a localised two-dimensional source produces $\sigma\propto\ln r$. If $\Phi$
is proportional to $\sigma$, then

\[
\Phi(r)\propto\ln r,
\qquad
g(r)\propto\frac{1}{r},
\qquad
v_c^2=r\frac{d\Phi}{dr}\simeq\text{constant}.
\]

A regularised logarithmic potential,

\[
\Phi_h(r)=\frac{V_\infty^2}{2}
\ln\!\left(\frac{r^2+r_c^2}{r_0^2}\right),
\]

gives

\[
v_h^2(r)=V_\infty^2\frac{r^2}{r^2+r_c^2}.
\]

The corresponding clock-rate field is

\[
A(r)=A_0\exp\!\left(\frac{\Phi_h(r)}{c^2}\right)
=A_0\left(\frac{r^2+r_c^2}{r_0^2}\right)^{V_\infty^2/(2c^2)},
\]

This establishes the time-dilation profile required by the fitted curve. It
does not derive $V_\infty$, $r_c$, or the screen source. The arbitrary
reference length $r_0$ changes only the potential zero and is absorbed into
$A_0$; it does not affect the acceleration.

### 5.2 Correct halo nomenclature

The velocity law above is the circular-speed law of a **cored logarithmic halo
potential**. It is not exactly the standard pseudo-isothermal density profile.
For a standard pseudo-isothermal sphere,

\[
\rho_{\rm pISO}(r)=\frac{\rho_0}{1+(r/r_c)^2},
\]

and

\[
v_{\rm pISO}^2(r)=4\pi G\rho_0r_c^2
\left[1-\frac{r_c}{r}\arctan\!\left(\frac r{r_c}\right)\right].
\]

The pilot comparison code incorrectly labelled the cored-logarithmic velocity
law as `ISO`. The exact identity found in that pilot is therefore an identity
between the logarithmic-SVT $k_2$ branch and a cored logarithmic halo, not a
standard pISO halo. The pISO comparison must be corrected and rerun before any
comparative conclusion is retained.

For a spherical interpretation, the effective density producing the
cored-logarithmic circular speed is

\[
\rho_{\log}(r)=
\frac{V_\infty^2}{4\pi G}
\frac{r^2+3r_c^2}{(r^2+r_c^2)^2}.
\]

This effective spherical density is a representation of the force law, not a
claim that the physical source must be spherical dark matter. The screen model
must derive its own geometry.

## 6. What the exploratory galaxy calculation says

The calculation in `/tmp/ssv-screen-pilot-223/` is an independent SPARC refit,
not an exact reconstruction of the source paper's THINGS analysis. It used
eleven overlapping galaxies, three fixed stellar mass-to-light scenarios, and
all 32 hierarchical combinations of the constant, $k_1/k_2$, linear, and
quadratic terms.

Under the primary baryonic assumption:

- the six-parameter published expression was not selected for any galaxy;
- the $k_2$ branch was necessary in the $\Delta\mathrm{AICc}\leq2$ confidence
  set for 10/11 galaxies;
- the linear term was necessary for 5/11;
- the constant term was necessary for 2/11;
- the quadratic term was necessary for 1/11;
- the $k_1$ branch was necessary for 0/11;
- the best common equation structure was `C_k2_L`, using four rather than six
  parameters;
- the complete expression improved raw $\chi^2$ by at most 3.558 over the
  selected reductions, and by less than 0.001 in six galaxies.

The statistical conclusion is that a cored-logarithmic radial component is
useful in this small fixed-baryon exercise. It does **not** identify the
physical source as a screen. The baryonic-mass sensitivity, local parameter
covariance, restricted sample, and corrected pISO comparison all remain
material qualifications.

## 7. Physical interpretation by scale

### 7.1 Individual matter and ordinary gravity

Encoded mass loads the screen even when stationary. Relocation adds continual
writes. Update drag is universal clock slowing rather than ordinary friction;
its gradient generates acceleration. A viable theory must recover the tested
Newtonian/weak-GR field and must not predict anomalous secular decay of stable
orbits.

### 7.2 Galaxies

A galaxy continually rewrites its screen representation through stellar
orbits, differential rotation, gas circulation, turbulence, accretion, star
formation, and feedback. A stationary macroscopic density can therefore
coexist with continual microscopic writing.

The bright central bulge, bar, ring, or putative stellar torus is not itself a
pseudo-isothermal or logarithmic halo. Its observed baryonic gravity belongs in
the disk/bulge mass model. It may nevertheless contribute strongly to screen
writing. The screen response, rather than the luminous torus, would be the
extended field.

Coherent standing-wave interference is **not required**. The earlier toy
emitter experiments showed that many uncorrelated coherent amplitudes wash out
rather than build a stable galaxy-scale pattern. Information loading is
proposed specifically as a positive/additive alternative. Resonant modes remain
an optional later hypothesis and must not be made load-bearing without a
derived coherence mechanism.

A central black hole cannot be the universal pump: dark-matter-like effects
occur in dwarf galaxies without a required central black hole and on cluster
scales. Black holes may affect a local screen mode but cannot be the sole
source.

### 7.3 Clusters and shared screen modes

If neighbouring systems couple to the same correlated screen mode, their
effective field may be collective rather than a linear sum of isolated galaxy
profiles. "In tune" must be replaced by a measurable synchronization or
overlap condition, such as a shared phase mode, correlation time, propagation
length, or coupled update spectrum.

The hypothesis must predict rather than retrofit:

- when separate galaxy responses merge into a cluster response;
- how the response behaves during a cluster collision;
- whether the screen field follows galaxies, collisionally shocked gas, the
  integrated history, or some calculable combination;
- the resulting weak- and strong-lensing maps.

### 7.4 Global screen and effective subsystem screens

The intended ontology has one global holographic screen associated with the
observable universe. Any physical state change within its boundary changes the
global encoding, independently of an external observer's description of other
systems. Galaxy and cluster "screens" are therefore shorthand for correlated,
approximately separable regions or modes of that one encoding.

This interpretation leaves two open mathematical problems:

1. how a bulk location and motion map to screen degrees of freedom without
   violating observer consistency; and
2. how effective patches compose without double-counting their information or
   gravitational response.

A finite global information capacity may also impose a maximum scale for
coherently represented structures. That is a possible cosmological consequence,
not part of the present galaxy-gravity derivation.

### 7.5 Candidate cosmological-redshift interpretation

A further working hypothesis is that the cosmological redshift of distant
galaxies is the measurable global effect of accumulated entropy/wake updating
of the universal screen. The screen's retained global record changes the
propagation or metric environment between emission and observation. Because
the background contribution is global and approximately homogeneous and
isotropic, it can change photon frequency without supplying a transverse
spatial gradient that bends the ray.

The absence of bending follows from homogeneity, not merely permanence:

\[
\nabla_\perp\Phi_{\rm global}\simeq0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{no global directional deflection}.
\]

Local departures from the global background still have spatial gradients and
therefore still produce gravitational lensing.

A time-dependent homogeneous lapse by itself is not sufficient. In

\[
ds^2=-A^2(t)c^2dt^2+d\mathbf x^2,
\]

the substitution $d\tau=A(t)dt$ removes $A(t)$; it is only a change of clock
coordinate and produces no invariant redshift between otherwise equivalent
comoving observers. A physical screen-redshift mechanism must therefore change
an additional observable structure, for example

\[
ds^2=-A^2(t)c^2dt^2+B^2(t)d\mathbf x^2,
\]

with a derived screen scale $B(t)$, or it must supply a covariant photon--screen
energy-transfer/propagation law. The model must derive a relation of the form

\[
1+z=\mathcal Z\!\left[W_{\rm screen}(t_{\rm obs}),
W_{\rm screen}(t_{\rm em})\right]
\]

rather than identifying redshift with entropy by name.

This branch must reproduce, with one fixed law, the low-redshift distance
relation, achromatic redshift, the observed stretching of transient light
curves, surface-brightness and distance-duality relations, the microwave
background spectrum and temperature history, large-scale standard-ruler
measurements, and redshift drift. A path-loss or scattering model that merely
reduces photon energy is insufficient if it blurs images or fails the required
time stretching. No such derivation has yet been supplied; the idea is recorded
here as a discriminating cosmological extension of screen memory.

#### Consequences for inferred cosmic age and size

If screen evolution supplies any non-zero fraction of cosmological redshift,
then redshift is no longer a direct proxy for geometric expansion alone. A
schematic decomposition is

\[
\frac{d\ln(1+z)}{dt}
=H_{\rm geom}(t)+\Gamma_{\rm wake}(t),
\]

where $H_{\rm geom}$ denotes the contribution from evolution of the spatial
metric and $\Gamma_{\rm wake}$ the additional screen contribution. The usual
age and distance integrals cannot be applied to the measured $z$ by simply
calling their sum the expansion rate. The emission time and radial distance
must instead be calculated from the full screen metric or photon-propagation
law.

This could substantially change the inferred age, lookback times, horizon
scale, and physical size associated with the observable universe. The direction
of the change is not fixed by the qualitative hypothesis: an added redshift per
unit path can make a source closer at fixed $z$, while a reduced geometric
expansion rate can increase the inferred expansion age. Claims that the
universe is specifically older, younger, larger, smaller, static, or eternal
must wait for the derived $\Gamma_{\rm wake}$ and distance law.

The reconstruction must be checked simultaneously against independent cosmic
chronometers and stellar ages, standard candles and rulers, background
radiation, primordial abundance constraints, structure growth, and the direct
redshift-drift sign and magnitude. Reinterpreting redshift requires a global
cosmological refit; changing the age formula alone would be inconsistent.

#### Candidate capacity scale and observational separation

The signal speed $c$ and a measured deep-space energy density $u_\infty$ can
help set a screen-throughput scale. Their direct product is an energy flux,

\[
\mathcal F_\infty=u_\infty c,
\]

not yet an update rate. Converting it to writes per screen area per time
requires an independently derived energy or information cost per write and a
screen cell area. If $G$ is also admitted, one can form the dimensional rate

\[
\Gamma_u\sim\sqrt{\frac{G u_\infty}{c^2}},
\]

but dimensional availability does not establish that this is the physical
screen-update law or fix its dimensionless coefficient. The definition of
"deep-space energy density" must distinguish vacuum, radiation, matter, and
the proposed screen contribution, and must not import a value inferred by
assuming the cosmology being tested.

Quasars can extend a test to large redshift, but their spectral redshift is not
an independent distance. A usable analysis needs a separately calibrated
quasar candle or ruler, reverberation distance, lensing/time-delay distance, or
quasar/Lyman-$\alpha$ standard-ruler measurement, with its calibration and
cosmological dependencies explicit.

The redshift components combine multiplicatively,

\[
1+z_{\rm obs}=
(1+z_{\rm geom})(1+z_{\rm wake})(1+z_{\rm pec})(1+z_{\rm grav}),
\]

and become approximately additive only when every contribution is small.
Consequently a linear distance--redshift term cannot be assigned uniquely to
entropy loss: geometric expansion itself is linear at lowest order. Nor can a
nonlinear term be assigned uniquely to expansion, because an accumulated
screen law such as $1+z_{\rm wake}=\exp(\kappa D)$ is also nonlinear at large
distance.

The identifiable test is therefore to derive $z_{\rm wake}(D)$ from the screen
capacity and write law, obtain distance from observables not defined by quasar
redshift alone, and fit the geometric and wake contributions jointly. Their
degeneracy must then be broken with transverse versus radial standard rulers,
transient time stretching, redshift drift, and any predicted dependence on the
integrated screen environment along the line of sight.

## 8. Lensing and emergent geometry

Rotation curves determine only a nonrelativistic potential. Lensing requires
the spatial metric as well as clock slowing. A suitable weak-field
parameterisation is

\[
ds^2=-\left(1+\frac{2\Phi}{c^2}\right)c^2dt^2
+\left(1-\frac{2\gamma\Phi}{c^2}\right)d\mathbf x^2.
\]

The screen theory must derive $\gamma\simeq1$ in tested regimes. Paper IV's
bare medium produced effectively the time half only, $\gamma\simeq0$, and is
falsified as a complete carrier of gravity. Saying that an updating screen
deforms is not yet a derivation of the spatial metric. The screen response must
produce both metric potentials with the observed relation.

## 9. Non-negotiable requirements

A viable screen theory must satisfy all of the following without
galaxy-by-galaxy changes to its laws.

1. **Causality:** screen disturbances propagate no faster than $c$.
2. **Local conservation:** information transfer and screen response have a
   consistent energy-momentum ledger.
3. **Observer consistency:** the write source cannot depend on an arbitrary
   coordinate velocity. If the screen defines a physical rest frame, its
   preferred-frame effects must satisfy observation.
4. **Positive, sign-definite attraction:** ordinary incoherent sources must not
   suffer the phase cancellation that killed the old wave mechanism.
5. **Equivalence:** all suitable clocks and freely falling matter respond to
   the same $A$.
6. **Newtonian and Solar-System limit:** recover $-GM/r$ and precision weak-GR
   tests in their domain.
7. **Galactic limit:** derive the transition to the cored-logarithmic response,
   rather than selecting it after seeing rotation curves.
8. **Baryonic scaling:** derive the observed mass-amplitude relation, including
   the approximate baryonic Tully--Fisher scaling $V_\infty^4\propto M_{\rm bar}$.
9. **Lensing:** derive the spatial metric and $\gamma\simeq1$.
10. **No unlimited age growth:** memory must not make otherwise identical older
    systems arbitrarily more gravitational.
11. **No excessive orbital drag:** update drag must be universal clock slowing,
    while any genuine dissipative backreaction remains below orbital bounds.
12. **Population-level parameters:** constants and response laws are global or
    derived from independently observed properties, not tuned separately to
    each rotation curve.
13. **Cluster prediction:** shared-screen behaviour and colliding-system
    lensing must follow from a fixed coupling rule.
14. **Cosmological consistency:** the global screen and effective subsystem
    patches must be defined without double-counting information or gravity. A
    proposed entropy/wake origin of cosmological redshift must be invariant,
    achromatic, non-blurring, and satisfy independent cosmic-clock, distance,
    background-radiation, standard-ruler, and redshift-drift tests.

Physical and mathematical possibility are necessary but not sufficient. The
theory must reduce its freedom by making predictions before new data are fit.

## 10. Failure modes to avoid

- **Rebranding a fit as a derivation.** A logarithmic curve does not establish
  a holographic source.
- **Confusing capacity, content, rate, and memory.** Each needs a separate
  definition and unit.
- **Counting coordinate speed.** A source proportional to $\rho|\mathbf v|$
  without a physical screen frame is observer-dependent.
- **Unbounded history dependence.** Integrating every write forever makes the
  field grow secularly.
- **Hidden friction.** Entropy production cannot silently drain orbital energy
  at an excluded rate.
- **Restoring coherent-wave assumptions.** The old interference source is
  falsified; positive screen loading is a different mechanism.
- **Assuming the lensing result.** $g_{00}$ alone supplies only the time sector.
- **Calling the cored-log profile pISO.** Their rotation laws differ.
- **Per-galaxy functional choice.** Selecting different laws after viewing each
  outcome destroys predictivity.
- **Using central black holes as universal pumps.** Dwarfs and clusters rule out
  that universal premise.
- **Using "shared" or "in tune" without an equation.** Correlation must have a
  measurable order parameter and threshold.

## 11. Discriminating predictions to seek

Once the source and response laws are specified, the first useful tests are:

1. the relation of $V_\infty$ and $r_c$ to baryonic mass, disk scale,
   angular momentum, gas fraction, star-formation rate, and independently
   estimated age;
2. whether write-rate observables improve predictions after baryonic mass and
   size are fixed;
3. whether galaxies with similar baryonic distributions but different internal
   activity have systematically different residual fields;
4. whether a screen-memory model predicts hysteresis after mergers or bursts;
5. whether environmental overlap predicts satellite and dwarf-galaxy dynamics
   without outcome-based sample selection;
6. whether one metric response fits rotation curves and lensing simultaneously;
7. whether cluster collisions place the inferred field where the fixed screen
   dynamics predicts;
8. whether Solar-System, binary-pulsar, gravitational-wave, and preferred-frame
   limits eliminate the required response coefficients;
9. whether one global wake-evolution law reproduces cosmological redshift,
   transient time stretching, distance relations, standard rulers, background
   radiation, and redshift drift without spatial refraction or image blurring.

Negative results are decisive. A successful rotation-curve fit is only
suggestive until the same fixed model passes independent lensing, scaling, and
local-gravity tests.

## 12. Resumption checklist

Resume the investigation in this order:

1. **Correct the comparison baseline.** Replace the pilot's mislabeled `ISO`
   velocity law with the genuine pISO $\arctan$ expression; rerun covariance and
   model selection; preserve the cored-log model under its correct name.
2. **Define the screen geometry.** Specify how a universal holographic screen
   maps bulk galaxy coordinates to an effective two-dimensional screen patch.
   Do not assume that a galaxy's disk plane is automatically the holographic
   screen.
3. **Define the source functional.** Express $\mathcal J_{\rm write}$ using
   covariant or screen-relative matter variables. Separate static content,
   relocation rate, and irreversible memory.
4. **Close the entropy statement.** Reconcile "all relocation writes" with
   Paper III's present exclusion of reversible relabelings, preferably through
   an explicit reduced-state/open-system calculation.
5. **Derive the static Green function.** Establish the boundary conditions and
   regularisation that yield the cored-logarithmic form. Derive, do not fit,
   $r_c$ or its scaling law.
6. **Derive amplitude scaling.** Obtain $V_\infty(M_{\rm bar},\ldots)$ and test
   the baryonic Tully--Fisher relation before adding activity variables.
7. **Specify memory regulation.** Derive relaxation, overwriting, transport, or
   saturation sufficiently to prevent unbounded age dependence.
8. **Complete the energy ledger.** Calculate the relationship between screen
   entropy production, stored screen energy, clock-rate slowing, and any
   orbital backreaction.
9. **Derive both metric sectors.** Calculate lensing rather than attaching the
   GR spatial term by hand.
10. **Pre-register galaxy tests.** Select galaxies by measurement quality and
    isolation before viewing model residuals. Compare the fixed screen model
    with baryons, NFW, Burkert, genuine pISO, MOND-like baselines, and the cored
    logarithmic phenomenology.
11. **Only then test shared cluster screens.** Define the correlation criterion
    first, then confront relaxed and merging clusters.
12. **Open the cosmological branch separately.** Define the invariant screen
    observable that changes photon frequency, prove why the homogeneous part
    causes no directional deflection, and pre-register the redshift and
    time-stretching laws before fitting distance data.
