# Post-preregistration addendum — hierarchical coherence screens

Status: **new hypothesis supplied after the issue-227 result; not used to alter
the frozen verdict**

## Hypothesis C5

Every sufficiently coherent physical system has a common effective screen.
Examples may range from entangled particles through molecules and living
systems to planets, stars, black holes, solar systems, galaxies, and clusters.
The number of effective screens can therefore be extremely large and their
domains can be nested or overlapping.

To remain compatible with C4, these are not independent copies of the universe
or new superluminal channels. They are effective subsystem screens—correlated
operator-algebra sectors or causal cuts—within the one global quantum-causal
state. A local operation still cannot change a spacelike remote marginal.

## Required mathematical closure

Let `S_X` denote the effective screen of coherent system `X`. C5 needs rules
for:

1. **Existence:** a basis-independent coherence measure and lifetime threshold
   deciding when `S_X` exists on the response timescale;
2. **nesting:** when `X` is a coherent part of `Y`, specify whether
   `S_X subset S_Y`, how their causal cuts relate, and how partially overlapping
   systems are treated;
3. **one-count capacity:** an underlying site or gate is charged once in the
   fundamental ledger even if several effective screens describe it;
4. **composition:** determine which screen, or weighted combination of screens,
   controls a particle's local availability;
5. **formation and dissolution:** state how screen records move when coherence
   is created, lost, split, or merged without secular energy leakage;
6. **observables:** fix all thresholds and weights globally, not separately for
   each molecule, galaxy, satellite, or cluster.

“Coherent system” cannot be defined merely as any chosen collection of degrees
of freedom: arbitrary tensor factorizations would create arbitrary screens and
destroy predictive content. The criterion must select dynamically persistent,
operationally identifiable correlations.

## Consequences for H-SSV-II

C5 offers a natural vocabulary for a dwarf galaxy sharing a host screen and
for a galaxy sharing a cluster screen. It also makes an isolated low-coherence
system potentially different from an otherwise similar embedded coherent
system. However, it does not yet determine the sign, weight, radial kernel, or
update rate of the enclosing-screen contribution.

The issue-227 `PHENOMENOLOGY ONLY` verdict therefore remains unchanged. A
future hierarchy audit should preregister a host/satellite/cluster sample and
test whether response changes track independently measured coherence or
binding proxies, without using rotation residuals to define screen membership.
