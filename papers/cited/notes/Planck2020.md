# Planck2020 — citation evidence

N. Aghanim et al. (Planck Collaboration), *Planck 2018 results. VI.
Cosmological parameters*, Astronomy & Astrophysics **641**, A6 (2020).

Primary source: arXiv:1807.06209 · DOI:
10.1051/0004-6361/201833910 · local PDF sha256 `8e172730faf07c9f`.

## SSV usage

SSV-I cites Planck for the statement that roughly 95% of the inferred cosmic
energy budget in base `\Lambda`CDM is non-baryonic dark matter or dark energy.
SSV-II `main.tex:2081–2087` cites Planck for the present cosmological bound
`\sum m_\nu<0.12 eV` and treats a future cosmological sum near `1 eV` as a
falsifier.

## Source result and explanation

The abstract gives `\Omega_m=0.315\pm0.007`,
`\Omega_c h^2=0.120\pm0.001`, `\Omega_b h^2=0.0224\pm0.0001`, and
`H_0=67.4\pm0.5 km s^{-1} Mpc^{-1}` under base `\Lambda`CDM.  Thus
`\Omega_b\simeq0.049`, leaving about 95.1% of the flat-model budget in cold
dark matter plus dark energy.  This supports SSV-I's explicitly
model-qualified “roughly 95%” wording.

Section 7.5.1, Eq. (63b), printed p. 48, gives the 95%-confidence constraint
`\sum m_\nu<0.12 eV` for `Planck TT,TE,EE+lowE+lensing+BAO`. The conclusion,
printed p. 62, states its assumptions compactly:

> Allowing for a free degenerate active neutrino mass, and combining with BAO
> measurements, we obtain the tight 95% constraint on the sum of the masses
> \(\sum m_\nu<0.12\) eV.

This is a cosmological, model- and data-combination-dependent upper limit, not
a direct laboratory measurement. The abstract also frames the result as a
single-parameter extension to base `\Lambda`CDM combined with BAO.

**Verdict: `OK`.** Both the model-qualified cosmic-budget statement and the
quoted `0.12 eV` mass-sum bound are supported. SSV should retain the qualifier
that these are cosmological-model inferences, and that the mass bound is the
95% Planck-plus-BAO constraint under the specified model.
