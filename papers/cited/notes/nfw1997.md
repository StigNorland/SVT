## Source result and explanation

The abstract, printed p. 493 (PDF p. 2), describes the simulation result:

> We use high-resolution N-body simulations to study the equilibrium density
> profiles of dark matter halos in hierarchically clustering universes. We
> find that all such profiles have the same shape, independent of halo mass,
> of initial density fluctuation spectrum, and of the values of the
> cosmological parameters.

The defining profile is then given on printed p. 494 (PDF p. 3), Eq. (1):

\[
\frac{\rho(r)}{\rho_{\rm crit}}
=\frac{\delta_c}{(r/r_s)(1+r/r_s)^2}.
\]

Here \(r_s\) is the scale radius and \(\delta_c\) the characteristic density.
The paper later derives the corresponding circular-velocity curve in Eq. (3),
\(V_c(r)=\sqrt{GM(r)/r}\), and reports that the profile fits simulated halos
over about two decades in radius.

**Verdict: `OK`.** This is the primary source of the NFW halo profile and
supports identifying an “NFW halo” comparison model. The broader statement
that dark-matter halos are the standard explanation of observed flat curves
is contextual consensus rather than a result established by this simulation
paper alone; the numerical quality of SSV's M31 fit remains SSV's own result.
