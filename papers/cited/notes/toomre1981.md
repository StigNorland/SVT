D. Lynden-Bell (eds.), *The Structure and Evolution of Normal Galaxies*
(Cambridge University Press, 1981), pp. 111–136.

Official source record and abstract:

## SSV usage

SSV-VI `main.tex:305–310` identifies the trailing spiral structure in its
self-gravitating baryonic simulation as swing amplification. It then observes
that its simulation has no central driver and concludes that no central driver
is needed for that result.

## Accessible primary abstract

The ADS abstract directly identifies the physical combination responsible for
the mechanism:

> The amplification is shown to result from a combination of galactic shear
> flow, epicyclic shaking and self-gravity, and to be characterized by
> oscillations

It describes the resulting arms as more tightly wound and denser than the
purely kinematic response, and goes on to discuss unstable spiral modes and
bar-making instabilities. The chapter's worked setup is a thin stellar disk
subject to weak, transient external quadrupole disturbances, so the source
does require a seed. Swing amplification means that the disk's shear and
self-gravity amplify a perturbation; it does not mean a perturbation is
created from literally nothing.

**Verdict: `OK` with seed scope.** The source directly supports identifying
the collective shear–epicycle–self-gravity mechanism as swing amplification.
It does not itself prove SSV's separate “no central driver” statement. That
statement rests on the documented SSV run, whose seed is particle shot noise,
not on this citation.
