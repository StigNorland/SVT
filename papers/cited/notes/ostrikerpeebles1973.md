## SSV usage

SSV-VI `main.tex:305–310` describes the bar plus trailing arms arising in its
baryons-only \(N\)-body disk as the Ostriker–Peebles instability. SSV's
measured amplitudes and morphology come from its own simulation.

## Source result and explanation

The abstract, printed p. 467 (PDF p. 1), reports that disk-like numerical
models were:

> found to be rapidly and grossly unstable to barlike modes. These modes cause
> an increase in random kinetic energy, with approximate stability being
> reached

The paper finds approximate stability after the rotational-to-gravitational
energy ratio falls to \(t\simeq0.14\), and shows that an added spherical halo
stabilizes the disk. Its result is specifically a large-scale bar instability
of cold, rotation-supported flattened systems.

**Verdict: `OK`.** The citation correctly identifies the classical bar
instability relevant to SSV's baryons-only disk. The 1973 paper does not
establish SSV's particular trailing-arm pitch evolution, nor does naming the
instability independently validate the numerical implementation.
