## Source result and explanation

The paper contrasts monotonically increasing entropy with its coarse-grained
“apparent complexity.” In the motivating coffee-cup example (p. 2):

> the coffee cup system starts out at a state of low complexity, and that the
> complexity first increases and then decreases over time.

The authors then test that proposed pattern with a two-dimensional cellular
automaton. For interacting particles they report numerical evidence of an
intermediate maximum; for non-interacting particles they prove that their
apparent-complexity measure never becomes large. This is an example and an
initial quantification, not a theorem that every named complexity measure is
non-monotone.

**Verdict: `OK`.** The citation supports SSV's contrast between monotone
entropy and rise-then-fall complexity, provided the sentence is read as a
general qualitative distinction rather than a universal theorem about
Bennett logical depth or Lloyd–Pagels thermodynamic depth.
