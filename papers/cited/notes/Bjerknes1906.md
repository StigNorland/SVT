V. Bjerknes, *Fields of Force* (Columbia University Press, 1906).

Open public-domain scan:
`https://archive.org/details/fieldsofforcecou00bjer`. No DOI is known; the

## SSV usage

SSV-II `main.tex:2444–2464`, SSV-IV `main.tex:568–576`, and SSV-VII-b
`main.tex:202–215` attribute an exact volume-rate force
\[
\langle F\rangle=-{\rho_0\over4\pi r^2}
\langle\dot V_1\dot V_2\rangle
\]
to Bjerknes. The later papers retain the historical mechanism while recording
that SSV's far-zone gravitational use is falsified.

## Primary result

Lecture 3, printed pp. 31–32, states:

> between bodies pulsating in the opposite phase there is an apparent
> repulsion, the force being proportional to the product of the two
> intensities of pulsation

It continues that the force is proportional to inverse distance squared;
same-phase pulsators attract. The derivation is for synchronous pulsators in
an incompressible hydrodynamic field. The book does not give the exact
\(\rho_0/(4\pi)\) volume-velocity normalization quoted by SSV, nor justify
carrying the near-field law into a retarded compressible far zone.

**Verdict: `MISATTRIBUTED` for the exact equation.** Bjerknes directly
supports the phase sign, bilinearity, and inverse-square structure. A later
secondary-Bjerknes derivation is needed for SSV's normalized formula; the
1906 citation alone is insufficient for that equality.
