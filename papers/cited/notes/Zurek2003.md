# Zurek2003 — citation evidence

W. H. Zurek, *Decoherence, einselection, and the quantum origins of the
classical*, Rev. Mod. Phys. **75**, 715–775 (2003).

Primary source: arXiv:quant-ph/0105127 · DOI:
10.1103/RevModPhys.75.715 · local PDF sha256 `d3e5276d515209a3`.

## SSV usage

SSV-III `main.tex:1375–1385` compares its wake argument to decoherence and
einselection: event information becomes entangled with environmental degrees
of freedom, the subsystem is no longer pure, global unitarity remains, and a
local agent cannot implement the full reversal.

## Source result and explanation

The abstract, printed p. 715 (PDF p. 1), states:

> Decoherence is caused by the interaction with the environment which in
> effect monitors certain observables of the system, destroying coherence
> between the pointer states corresponding to their eigenvalues. This leads
> to environment-induced superselection or einselection, a quantum process
> associated with selective loss of information.

It also says that correlations with pointer states spread through the
environment and that reduced density matrices describe a subsystem of a
larger combined system. In Sec. I.C, printed p. 718 (PDF p. 4), the review
retains unitary Schrödinger evolution for the undivided universe while
treating its interacting subsystems as open systems. This is the distinction
SSV invokes between a globally pure evolution and locally inaccessible
environmental information.

**Verdict: `OK`.** The review supports the analogy to environment-induced
entanglement, loss of local coherence, environmental records, and global
unitarity. It does not prove SSV's stronger medium-specific assertion that no
local apparatus can ever prepare the boundary conditions required to reverse
a wake; that operational impossibility remains an SSV argument.
