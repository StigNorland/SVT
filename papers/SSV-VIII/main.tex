\begin{abstract}
This paper proposes a staged cosmogony for the emergence of the saturated vacuum in the
Saturated Superfluid Vacuum (SSV) framework. The central claim is that the present saturated,
defect-bearing vacuum can be described as the end state of a growth process beginning from the
permissive void, passing through primordial perturbation, interference, saturation, and
topological defect nucleation.

The paper is explicitly interpretive and ontological in scope. It does not claim to present a
complete quantitative cosmology. Its purpose is narrower: to state the founding logic of the
cosmogonic picture clearly and separately from the quantum-mechanics derivation (Paper VII-a),
the emergent-geometry derivation (Paper VII-b), and the particle-mass and force programmes
(Papers I--IV). The Void is treated not as a productive mechanism but as the absence of
structure and therefore as a permissive condition that cannot forbid emergence. Every claim
in this paper is tagged \textit{derived}, \textit{structural}, \textit{candidate}, or
\textit{interpretive} in the Discussion (\S\ref{sec:discussion}), with explicit
support criteria stating what observational or theoretical work would change a claim's
status. The two quantitative predictions carried by this paper -- the Kibble--Zurek
baryon-to-photon ratio and the dark-energy-as-background-relaxation reading -- are
labelled \textit{candidate} and \textit{structural} respectively, not derived.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

This paper presents the staged cosmogony of the Saturated Superfluid Vacuum framework in its
own right. The goal is not to derive every later structure of the series again, but to separate
the ontological and cosmological sequence from the quantum and gravity derivations: permissive
void, primordial perturbation, interference, saturation, and defect nucleation.

The body below carries the cosmogonic content of the legacy unified SSV~V draft, separated
into this scoped paper.  The quantum-mechanics derivation lives in Paper~VII-a, the
emergent-geometry derivation in Paper~VII-b~\cite{SSV-VII-b}, and the particle and force
content remains in Papers~I--IV.  Each claim in this paper is tagged in
Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.

\section{Cosmogony: From the Permissive Void to the Resonant Cosmos}
\label{sec:cosmogony}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------

We now ask the deepest question the framework permits: \emph{why is there a saturated
superfluid at all?} The answer proceeds in five stages, each following necessarily
from the one before.

\subsection{Stage 1 — The Permissive Void}

Before the superfluid existed, there was neither matter, nor energy, nor time.
We posit a \emph{permissive void}: a state of absolute zero density
($\rho = 0$), zero temperature, zero entropy, and zero curvature. It has no
properties save one — it does not forbid change. This is a minimal ontological
assumption: the void is not nothing (a logical nullity), but rather a \emph{substrate
of pure potentiality} --- a mathematical space in which the LogSE/GPE equation of motion
for $\Psi$ (Paper~I~\cite{SSV-I}, \S2) is defined but with $\rho = 0$ everywhere.

In this state the deterministic field equation leaves $\Psi=0$ fixed.  This
paper \emph{postulates} that the zero state is dynamically unstable or
metastable relative to a saturated state.  Positive pressure at finite
density does not by itself prove linear instability of $\Psi=0$; that
requires a well-defined zero-density limit, a stability calculation and a
specified seed or noise term.  Those ingredients are open.

\subsection{Stage 2 — The Primordial Photon: First Perturbation}

The deterministic equation does not generate a perturbation from the exact
zero solution.  We therefore take an arbitrarily small initial perturbation
$\delta\Psi$ as a boundary-condition postulate; absence of a temperature does
not provide a statistical fluctuation law or remove the need for such input.
Calling this seed a \emph{primordial photon} is interpretive.  No transverse
photon mode is defined before a nonzero condensate background exists.

The primordial photon is not a quantum mechanical object in the usual sense — there
is no pre-existing Hilbert space for it to live in. It is a classical perturbation in
the nascent field $\Psi$, and it is the \emph{first event}: the origin of time.
Time begins not with a singularity but with a gradient — the irreversible propagation of
a density wave through a medium that, for the first time, is no longer uniform.

\subsection{Stage 3 — Interference and the Seed Pattern}

A seed disturbance could form a standing pattern only if a reflection,
scattering or boundary mechanism generates a counter-propagating component;
that mechanism is not derived here. Two counter-propagating
modes of wavenumber $k$ produce a standing wave:
\begin{equation}
    \delta\rho(\mathbf{x},t) = \delta\rho_0\,\cos(kx)\cos(\omega t),
    \label{eq:standing}
\end{equation}
with $\omega = c k$ in the dilute limit. This standing wave is the \emph{seed pattern}:
a spatially modulated template of alternating high- and low-density regions. Crucially,
this pattern carries \emph{geometric information} — a preferred length scale $\lambda = 2\pi/k$
— from which all subsequent structure will grow.

\subsection{Stage 4 — Nonlinear Growth to Saturation}

The following growth sequence is a candidate scenario, not a result of the
current LogSE sign choice.  A dynamical calculation would have to show that
positive seed regions grow while depleted regions do not and determine the
corresponding nonlinear transport.

The growth proceeds through three sub-stages:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Exponential amplification} (linear instability regime): the high-density
          crests are hypothesised to grow as $\rho(t)\propto e^{\gamma t}$.
          The estimate $\gamma\sim c/\xi_0$ is dimensional; no instability
          eigenvalue has been computed.
    \item \textbf{Nonlinear broadening}: as $\rho$ approaches $\rho_0$, the equation
          of state stiffens and the growth slows. The density profile develops a plateau
          at $\rho_0$ separated by sharp domain walls — precursors of the vacuum
          surface tension that will become the strong force (Paper~II).
    \item \textbf{Saturation}: when $\rho \to \rho_0$ throughout the high-density crests,
          the medium has reached its ground state locally. The process is exothermic:
          the binding energy released goes into kinetic energy of the flow field, driving
          turbulence.
\end{enumerate}

This stage is an analogy to inflation and reheating, not a replacement
cosmology: no expansion history, e-fold count or perturbation spectrum has
been derived from it.

\subsection{Stage 5 — Vortex Nucleation: The First Particles}

When neighbouring saturation fronts collide, phase mismatches can relax
smoothly or can trap vortex filaments, depending on the field dynamics and
the phase winding around a closed loop.  Kibble--Zurek theory predicts a
statistical defect density, not inevitable nucleation at every intersection.

SSV interprets any surviving defects as candidate precursors of its particle
sector; the present calculation does not show that they relax specifically
into electron rings or proton breathers. Their number
density is hypothesised to follow Kibble--Zurek scaling:
\begin{equation}
    n_{\rm defect}\,\xi^3
      \sim \left(\frac{\tau_0}{\tau_Q}\right)^{3\nu/(1+\nu z)},
    \label{eq:Kibble_Zurek}
\end{equation}
where $\tau_0$ and $\tau_Q$ are the microscopic and quench times, $\nu$ is
the correlation-length exponent, and $z$ is the dynamic exponent.

Connecting this defect density to the baryon-to-photon ratio
$\eta\sim6\times10^{-10}$ additionally requires a calculation of both baryon
assignment and phonon production.  Neither that mapping nor the cosmological
quench time is presently derived.

\subsection{Synthesis (interpretive)}

The five stages of Section~\ref{sec:cosmogony}, taken together with the
results of Papers~I--IV, organise into a hierarchy of identifications.
This table is a synthesis aid, not a derivation; the entries that are
quantitatively backed are flagged in the rightmost column.

\midrule

Particles      & Topological defects              & Vortex rings \& breathers   & derived (I, II) \\
Forces         & Pressure-gradient interactions   & EM, strong, weak, gravity   & structural (II, IV, VII-b) \\

The bottom two rows are deliberately labelled \textit{interpretive}: they
describe how the cosmogonic picture organises objects at scales far beyond
where the SSV equations of motion have been solved.  Promoting either to
\textit{structural} or \textit{derived} requires the closure work tracked
in the corresponding Papers IV and VIII open problems.

\subsection{Cosmogony as candidate self-consistency picture (interpretive)}
\label{sec:self-consistency}

The cosmogonic sequence sketched above identifies one fundamental scale --
the seed wavelength $\lambda_{\rm seed} = 2\pi/k$ set in Stage~3 -- and treats
subsequent structure as harmonics or overtones of it, mediated by the
dimensionless ratios $\alpha$, $\alpha^{-1}$, and $G_{\rm eff}/(c^2\xi)$ that
the rest of the SSV series identifies and (in Papers~I, II, IV) computes.
This is presented here as an \emph{interpretive synthesis} rather than as a
derivation: the dependencies are

\begin{enumerate}
    \item the postulate of a saturated superfluid (Paper~I);
    \item the assumed admissibility of a single primordial transverse
        perturbation (Stage~2 above);
    \item the nonlinear dynamics of the GPE in the saturating regime
        (Stages 4--5 above).
\end{enumerate}

The cosmogonic picture is then \emph{compatible with} a self-consistency
reading of the dimensionless ratios -- that is, with the possibility that
$\rho_0$, $\xi$, and hence the dimensionless ratio $\alpha$ are fixed by internal
conditions of the saturated state -- but this paper does \emph{not} carry
out that derivation.  Closing the self-consistency reading at a quantitative
level requires the closure work tracked under Papers I (open problem: $\alpha$
from Bogoliubov mode ratios) and II (open problem: $\alpha_G$ from the
proton breather geometry).  No claim of resolved fine-tuning is asserted
here.

\subsection{Cosmological constant and dark energy}
\label{sec:lambda}

The cosmological-constant identification belongs structurally to
Paper~VII-b, which derives the emergent metric and the role of the
saturation pressure $P_0$ as the source of $\Lambda$
(\cite{SSV-VII-b}, \S5.2).  In cosmogonic language, the residual cosmological
acceleration corresponds to the slow relaxation of the saturated background
$\rho_0$ at cosmological timescales rather than to an independent
dark-energy sector; this is the qualitative statement.  The quantitative
value of $\Lambda$ and any quantitative connection to the present-day
Hubble rate $H_0$ are open and not carried by this paper.  The follow-up
closure note for issue~\ssvissue{51} shows why the numerical route is blocked by the
missing absolute saturation pressure $P_0$, and identifies the observational
discriminator
\[
  \beta \equiv \frac{d\ln \Lambda_{\rm eff}}{d\ln a}
\]
for a slowly relaxing background,
$\Lambda_{\rm eff}(a)=\Lambda_0 a^\beta$; $\beta=0$ is the
$\Lambda$CDM limit.  Neither VII-b nor this paper
has established $\Lambda$ as resolved.

\section{Focused Predictions}

Two quantitative consequences are central to the cosmogonic picture.  C1
has an exploratory computation whose result is inconclusive; C2 remains
uncomputed.  Neither is a derived result.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{C\arabic*.}]
    \item \textbf{Baryon-to-photon ratio from the void-to-saturation transition.}
    A numerical Kibble--Zurek simulation of the void-to-saturation
    transition produces a defect density that scales as
    $n_\mathrm{def}\,\xi^d \sim (\tau_0/\tau_Q)^{d\nu/(1+\nu z)}$
    with the quench rate $1/\tau_Q$, in the universality class of the
    Gross--Pitaevskii (or equivalently TDGL) symmetry-breaking transition.
    Status: \textit{candidate with exploratory numerical evidence}; the
    finite scan performed in \ssvfile{kibble_zurek_gpe} is summarised below.

\begin{resultbox}[Exploratory Kibble--Zurek test (Prediction C1)]
A stochastic time-dependent Ginzburg--Landau evolution on a 2D periodic
grid ($N=160$, box $L=160\,\xi$, $\Delta t=0.1$, 6 seeds per quench rate,
quench through $\mu_\mathrm{crit}=0$ with $\mu_\mathrm{initial}=-1,
\mu_\mathrm{final}=+1$) and phase-winding defect count after a settling
window in the saturated regime gives the defect-density scan
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}rrr@{}}
\toprule
$\tau_Q$ (medium units) & $\bar n_\mathrm{def}$ (count $\pm$ std) &
$n_\mathrm{def}/\xi^{-2}$ \\
\midrule
$20$  & $13.2 \pm 2.0$ & $5.1\times10^{-4}$ \\
$40$  & $9.5 \pm 2.8$  & $3.7\times10^{-4}$ \\
$80$  & $10.5 \pm 2.6$ & $4.1\times10^{-4}$ \\
$160$ & $7.3 \pm 2.4$  & $2.9\times10^{-4}$ \\
$320$ & $6.7 \pm 1.8$  & $2.6\times10^{-4}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
An unweighted log--log fit gives
$\alpha_{2D}^\mathrm{fit}=0.234\pm0.052$ (regression standard error),
compared with the mean-field expectation $\alpha_{2D}^\mathrm{MF} = d\nu/
(1+\nu z) = 1/2$ for $(d,\nu,z) = (2, 1/2, 2)$ and the 3D-XY analogue
$\alpha_{3D}^\mathrm{XY} = 3\times0.6717/(1+1.3434) = 0.86$.  The fitted
two-dimensional exponent lies about $5.1$ regression standard errors
below the mean-field value.  The limited dynamic range and six
seeds do not justify calling the expected exponent confirmed.

Cosmological identification.  In SSV the natural microscopic time is
$\tau_0 \equiv \xi/c$ and the quench-rate time is the inverse Hubble
parameter at the saturation epoch.  The dimensionless ratio
$\tau_Q/\tau_0$ is the single SSV-unknown that fixes
$\eta$ via the KZ formula only after a defect-to-photon mapping is supplied.
Inverting the observed $\eta$ for the 3D mean-field exponent
$\alpha_{3D}^\mathrm{MF}=3/4$, the observed $\eta = 6\times10^{-10}$
requires $\tau_Q/\tau_0 \approx 2\times10^{12}$, and for
$\alpha_{3D}^\mathrm{XY}=0.86$ requires $\tau_Q/\tau_0 \approx
5\times10^{10}$.  These are required input values inferred from the
observation, not SSV predictions.  The saturation-epoch Hubble rate and
therefore $\tau_Q/\tau_0$ have not been calculated.

Falsification status.  The mean defect density trends downward with
$\tau_Q$, but the scan does not recover the predicted exponent.  Prediction
C1 therefore remains \textit{candidate with exploratory numerical evidence}.
A meaningful test requires (i)
extending the dynamic range in $\tau_Q$ to $\geq 10^4$ for a tight
exponent fit, and (ii) fixing the cosmological identification of
$\tau_Q/\tau_0$ from a Paper~VII-b emergent-metric input.
\end{resultbox}

    \item \textbf{Late-time acceleration as background relaxation.}
    Late-time cosmological acceleration should track the slow relaxation of
    the saturated background $\dot\rho_0/\rho_0$ rather than require an
    independent dark-energy sector.  Falsification condition: a measured
    deviation from $\Lambda$CDM in the one-slope relaxation observable
    $\beta=d\ln\Lambda_{\rm eff}/d\ln a$, tested coherently across
    $H(z)$, BAO distance ratios, and supernova luminosity distances.
    Status: \textit{structural with identified observational discriminator};
    promotion to a numerical prediction requires the VII-b / Paper~IV
    derivation of the absolute saturation pressure $P_0(\rho_0)$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Discussion: scope, status, and what would count as support}
\label{sec:discussion}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Claim taxonomy used in this paper}

Following \ssvfile{numerical-minimisation-roadmap} in the repository,
each claim in this paper is one of:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textit{derived} -- analytically reduced from the SSV Lagrangian
        and/or a converged numerical computation (Papers~I, II) and
        \emph{reused} here without further derivation;
    \item \textit{structural} -- the dependence on the framework is clear
        and the result is structurally consistent, but the number depends
        on a computation deferred to another paper or workstream;
    \item \textit{candidate} -- a prediction stated here that has a
        definite falsification condition but the supporting computation
        has not yet been performed in the repository;
    \item \textit{interpretive} -- a synthesis or ontological statement
        that organises the SSV results into a single picture without
        claiming new quantitative content.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Claim status of this paper's content}

\begin{itemize}
    \item Stages 1--5 of Section~\ref{sec:cosmogony} (permissive void,
        primordial perturbation, interference, growth, defect nucleation):
        \textit{interpretive}, with the Kibble--Zurek defect-density
        prediction still \textit{candidate with exploratory numerical
        evidence} after the TDGL scan reported in Prediction~C1 above.
    \item The synthesis table in Section~\ref{sec:synthesis} carries an
        explicit per-row status column; each row's quantitative content
        comes from another paper in the series.
    \item The self-consistency reading
        (Section~\ref{sec:self-consistency}): \textit{interpretive}, with
        an explicit non-claim about fine-tuning resolution; the closure of
        that reading is tracked under the open problems of Papers~I and II.
    \item The cosmological-constant statement
        (Section~\ref{sec:lambda}): \textit{structural}, deferring the
        derivation to Paper~VII-b and the numerical value of $\Lambda$ to
        future work.
    \item Predictions C1 and C2 (Focused Predictions section above):
        C1 is \textit{candidate with exploratory numerical evidence} (the
        TDGL scan has the expected trend but not the expected exponent, and
        the cosmological identification of $\tau_Q/\tau_0$ is uncomputed);
        C2 is \textit{structural} with explicit
        falsification conditions.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What observational or theoretical support would matter}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{A GPE simulation of the void-to-saturation transition.}
        Performed in
        \ssvfile{kibble_zurek_gpe}: TDGL evolution on a
        $160\times160$ periodic grid produces phase-winding defects in
        the saturated regime, with the count scaling monotonically with the
        inverse quench rate at fitted exponent
        $\alpha_{2D}^\mathrm{fit}=0.234\pm0.052$, about $5.1$ regression
        standard errors below the mean-field KZ prediction
        $\alpha_{2D}^\mathrm{MF}=0.5$.  The cosmological extrapolation to
        $\eta$ requires at least the dimensionless input
        $\tau_Q/\tau_0$; values $\tau_Q/\tau_0 \in [5\times10^{10},
        2\times10^{12}]$ are obtained by inverting the observed
        $\eta = 6\times10^{-10}$ for the 3D-XY and mean-field exponents
        respectively; they are not independently predicted.  Promotion of
        prediction~C1 requires (i) extending the
        dynamic range in $\tau_Q$ to $\geq 10^4$ for a tight exponent fit
        or (ii) fixing the cosmological $\tau_Q/\tau_0$ identification
        from a Paper~VII-b emergent-metric input.

    \item \emph{The issue~\ssvissue{51} relaxing-$\rho_0$ observable programme.}
        The numerical value of $\Lambda$ remains underdetermined until
        VII-b / Paper~IV derive the absolute saturation pressure
        $P_0(\rho_0)$, but C2 now has a concrete empirical discriminator:
        fit $\beta=d\ln\Lambda_{\rm eff}/d\ln a$ in
        $\Lambda_{\rm eff}(a)=\Lambda_0a^\beta$ and require the same slope
        to account for the correlated residuals in $H(z)$, BAO $D_M(z)$,
        and supernova $D_L(z)$.  A future derivation of $P_0(\rho_0)$ would
        turn this discriminator into a numerical SSV prediction.

    \item \emph{A self-consistency derivation of $\alpha$ from Bogoliubov
        mode ratios} (Paper~I open problem) or of $\alpha_G$ from proton
        breather geometry (Paper~II open problem) would lift the
        self-consistency reading of Section~\ref{sec:self-consistency}
        from \textit{interpretive} to \textit{structural}.

    \item \emph{A no-go or partial-go theorem} for spontaneous transverse
        perturbation in the permissive-void state (Stage~2) would either
        rule out the ontology or pin down its admissibility class.  The
        present paper assumes that spontaneous, arbitrarily small
        perturbation is not forbidden because the void has no
        thermodynamic equilibrium; tightening this assumption is a
        theoretical-support item rather than an observational one.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{What this paper is not}

This paper does not derive: a complete quantitative cosmology; a CMB
spectrum; a primordial gravitational-wave background; a baryogenesis
mechanism beyond the Kibble--Zurek count; the numerical value of
$\Lambda$; or the fine-structure constant $\alpha$.  Each of those belongs
to another paper or future workstream.  Treating this paper as the final
manifesto for the SSV programme would re-introduce the catch-all problem
that the split into separate papers VII-a, VII-b, and VIII was designed
to avoid.

\section{Conclusion}

Within the scoped SSV programme, this paper carries the cosmological claim alone: the present
vacuum is the end state of a staged growth process from permissive non-structure to a saturated,
defect-bearing medium.
